And There Shall Be No More Curse

A narrative poem that follows God from before time began, through the cross, to the day when there shall be no more curse.

Download or open the MP3

Before the mountains were brought forth, before the first morning,

God was. Not waiting. Not lonely. Not lacking anything.

The Father loved the Son before the foundation of the world,

and the glory they shared is older than the stars.

Then, out of fullness and not need, He spoke the world awake,

and looked, and behold, it was very good.

He stooped to the dust and breathed,

and man became a living soul, borrowing every breath.

Before the mountains were brought forth, He is God.

He needs nothing. He gives all things.

And there shall be no more curse.

This God does not change.

Saul turned aside, and the kingdom was torn from him

as the hem of Samuel’s mantle tore,

but the Strength of Israel did not lie, nor turn as a man turns.

He fainteth not, neither is weary;

no chisel can carve Him, for God is a Spirit.

His ways are past finding out—

and still, the only begotten Son has declared Him.

Before the mountains were brought forth, He is God.

He needs nothing. He gives all things.

And there shall be no more curse.

His counsel stands. He does whatever He pleases;

none can stay His hand, or say unto Him, What doest thou?

He removes kings and sets up kings;

Nebuchadnezzar ate grass like an ox until he knew it.

Joseph’s brothers sold him for silver and meant it for evil;

God meant it unto good.

And one day wicked hands would take the Lamb

by the determinate counsel of God—

the sinners guilty, the purpose holy, the hand unstayed.

Before the mountains were brought forth, He is God.

He needs nothing. He gives all things.

And there shall be no more curse.

He telleth the number of the stars, and calleth them all by their names;

the very hairs of your head are all numbered.

Climb into heaven, He is there; make your bed in hell, He is there;

take the wings of the morning to the uttermost parts of the sea,

and even there His hand will lead you.

He knew the cross before the foundation of the world,

and knowing it forced no one’s hand.

So ask Him. Your Father knows what you need before you ask.

Before the mountains were brought forth, He is God.

He needs nothing. He gives all things.

And there shall be no more curse.

Then the serpent spoke, a hand reached out,

and the ground was cursed for our sake.

And still the seraphim cover their faces and cry,

Holy, holy, holy, is the LORD of hosts.

He is of purer eyes than to behold evil;

He will by no means clear the guilty.

Two road blocks stand on the way home:

a holiness no sinner can come near, a justice no sinner can satisfy,

and the wrath of God revealed from heaven against all ungodliness.

Before the mountains were brought forth, He is God.

He needs nothing. He gives all things.

And there shall be no more curse.

Yet the road shut from our side was opened from His.

His mercies are new every morning; they hold back what we have earned.

His grace gives what we never earned.

He is longsuffering, not willing that any should perish.

He commended His love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners,

Christ died for us—

and He did not lower His holiness one inch to do it.

He is just, and the justifier of him which believeth in Jesus.

Before the mountains were brought forth, He is God.

He needs nothing. He gives all things.

And there shall be no more curse.

The way through is not a road but a Person,

whose goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting.

In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God;

all things were made by Him.

He said, Before Abraham was, I am, and they took up stones.

Promised in Eden, foretold by the prophets, He is the Christ;

and Thomas, seeing Him, said, My Lord and my God—

and He did not correct him.

Before the mountains were brought forth, He is God.

He needs nothing. He gives all things.

And there shall be no more curse.

Then the Word was made flesh.

A virgin conceived by the Holy Ghost

and laid her firstborn son in a manger:

God, not ceasing to be God; man, truly man.

He grew. He hungered. He sat wearied by a well. He wept at a grave.

He was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin.

Made like unto His brethren, He came as our Kinsman

to make reconciliation for the sins of the people.

Before the mountains were brought forth, He is God.

He needs nothing. He gives all things.

And there shall be no more curse.

Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.

He was wounded for our transgressions;

He was made a curse for us, and the wrath we earned fell on Him.

He cried, It is finished,

and the veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom.

One offering, for ever; nothing can be added.

On the third day the tomb stood empty,

and above five hundred brethren saw Him alive at once.

Before the mountains were brought forth, He is God.

He needs nothing. He gives all things.

And there shall be no more curse.

A cloud received Him out of their sight,

and two men in white apparel said, This same Jesus shall so come.

Now He sits on the right hand of the Majesty on high,

with a name which is above every name,

ever living to make intercession,

building His church, preparing a place,

until His enemies be made His footstool.

Before the mountains were brought forth, He is God.

He needs nothing. He gives all things.

And there shall be no more curse.

The Spirit was there before the first light,

moving upon the face of the waters.

He is no wind or force: He is grieved, He wills, He speaks;

to lie to Him is to lie to God.

He moved holy men of God to speak;

He came upon David the shepherd from that day forward;

and through Joel, God promised, I will pour out my spirit upon all flesh.

Before the mountains were brought forth, He is God.

He needs nothing. He gives all things.

And there shall be no more curse.

At Pentecost the promise came, cloven tongues like as of fire.

Now He reproves the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment;

He gives the new birth, baptizes every believer into one body,

and seals them unto the day of redemption.

He makes His home within, and grows love, joy, peace;

He gives gifts for serving, and power to tell.

And it is the Spirit, with the bride, who says, Come.

Before the mountains were brought forth, He is God.

He needs nothing. He gives all things.

And there shall be no more curse.

Look honestly at the wall.

By one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin,

and so death passed upon all men.

The wages of sin is death, and we were dead in trespasses and sins;

all our righteousnesses are as filthy rags.

At Babel men made brick and said, Let us build us a tower

whose top may reach unto heaven. No tower ever has.

Even Nicodemus, a ruler, came by night to learn he must be born again.

Before the mountains were brought forth, He is God.

He needs nothing. He gives all things.

And there shall be no more curse.

So God took the wall away.

When we were enemies, we were reconciled to God by the death of His Son;

enmity turned to peace, and the work is finished.

All things are of God.

Two thieves hung beside the cross that day:

one railed, and one said, Lord, remember me.

The price was paid for every sin; one of them received it.

Behold, now is the day of salvation.

Before the mountains were brought forth, He is God.

He needs nothing. He gives all things.

And there shall be no more curse.

And those who receive it, God gathers.

He broke down the middle wall of partition

and made of twain one new man, Jew and Gentile, one body by one Spirit:

a temple of living stones, a royal priesthood, a bride,

branches in one Vine, salt in the earth, a city set on a hill.

Yet God hath not cast away His people which He foreknew;

the gifts and calling of God are without repentance.

Before the mountains were brought forth, He is God.

He needs nothing. He gives all things.

And there shall be no more curse.

So we are sent as ambassadors for Christ:

Be ye reconciled to God.

We go down into the water, buried with Him,

and rise to walk in newness of life.

We break the bread and drink the cup, showing the Lord’s death till He come;

the cup is the new testament in His blood,

and our sins and iniquities He remembers no more.

Elders feed the flock, and every gift builds up the body.

Before the mountains were brought forth, He is God.

He needs nothing. He gives all things.

And there shall be no more curse.

But there is an enemy in this story, and he is not God’s equal.

He was made, a creature, and he was lifted up with pride

and said in his heart, I will be like the most High.

He is a liar, and the father of it;

he accuses the brethren before our God day and night.

He could not touch Job past the line God drew.

The holy angels stood, and still minister to the heirs of salvation;

and when John fell at an angel’s feet, the angel said,

See thou do it not: worship God.

Before the mountains were brought forth, He is God.

He needs nothing. He gives all things.

And there shall be no more curse.

He cannot reach the throne, so he strikes at what bears God’s image.

He was a murderer from the beginning; Abel’s blood cried from the ground.

He pulls at marriage, sours work, bends rulers toward evil.

From Cain’s field to Pharaoh’s river, from Haman’s decree

to Herod’s sword in Bethlehem, he hunted the promised Seed.

In the wilderness he came to the Son Himself, and found no sin in Him.

But the word of our God shall stand for ever,

and God kept the line, the law, and every promise.

Before the mountains were brought forth, He is God.

He needs nothing. He gives all things.

And there shall be no more curse.

So God arms His people: Be strong in the Lord, and in the power of His might.

Truth for a girdle, righteousness for a breastplate,

feet shod with the preparation of the gospel of peace,

the shield of faith, the helmet of salvation,

and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God—

the same sword the Son lifted in the wilderness: It is written.

Submit to God; resist the devil, and he will flee from you.

Greater is He that is in you than he that is in the world,

and the gospel goes out to turn men from the power of Satan unto God.

Before the mountains were brought forth, He is God.

He needs nothing. He gives all things.

And there shall be no more curse.

At the cross the old word from Eden came true:

the serpent bruised the Seed’s heel, and the Seed bruised the serpent’s head.

Had the princes of this world known, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory.

There He spoiled principalities and powers and made a show of them openly,

nailing the handwriting against us to His cross.

The devil rages, knowing that he hath but a short time;

he will be bound, then cast into the lake of fire,

and every work of his destroyed.

Before the mountains were brought forth, He is God.

He needs nothing. He gives all things.

And there shall be no more curse.

Believers have read the thousand years in different ways,

yet all look for that blessed hope:

this same Jesus, coming in like manner, raising the dead, judging the world.

The Lord Himself shall descend from heaven with a shout;

the dead in Christ shall rise first, and we which are alive

shall be caught up to meet the Lord in the air,

and so shall we ever be with the Lord;

for God hath not appointed us to wrath.

Before the mountains were brought forth, He is God.

He needs nothing. He gives all things.

And there shall be no more curse.

Long before, God had brought Abram out under the stars

and passed between the pieces Himself, a burning lamp in the dark:

In thee shall all families of the earth be blessed.

Through Israel came the covenants, the oracles, and Christ according to the flesh.

He came unto His own, and His own received Him not;

so for a while the nation stands aside,

and the casting away of them is the reconciling of the world:

blindness in part, until the fulness of the Gentiles be come in.

Before the mountains were brought forth, He is God.

He needs nothing. He gives all things.

And there shall be no more curse.

Then the church caught up; then the time of Jacob’s trouble;

and even then a great multitude, which no man could number,

washed in the blood of the Lamb.

Then He comes, and every eye shall see Him;

Israel looks upon Him whom they pierced and mourns as for an only son,

and He reigns.

The books are opened before the great white throne;

but he that heareth His word, and believeth, shall not come into condemnation.

Then a new heaven and a new earth, and God Himself shall dwell with them.

He shall wipe away all tears; there shall be no more death.

The throne of God and of the Lamb shall be in it; His servants shall serve Him,

and they shall see His face.

All things are of God, who hath reconciled us to Himself by Jesus Christ.

Before the mountains were brought forth, He is God.

He needs nothing. He gives all things.

And there shall be no more curse.
